# Patient Money — plan, guardrails and first 30 days

**One line:** a free, honest "start now vs. later" calculator and two-minute lessons that help 18–25 year olds see what time does for their money, and what risk looks like, so they make their own call.

**Positioning:** the anti-hype alternative to finfluencers. No stock tips, no promised returns, a source for every number. "Your money, your risk, your call."

## Staying on the right side of the law

*This is a summary of the rules to plan around, not legal advice. Book one hour with a securities attorney before the first paid partnership.*

**Why education, done this way, is not "investment advice":** the Investment Advisers Act excludes bona fide publications. The Supreme Court (*Lowe v. SEC*, 1985) read that as content that is **impersonal** (not tailored to one person), **disinterested** (not promoting something you profit from) and offered on a **regular schedule** to the general public (not timed to hype a trade). Everything here is designed to fit:

| We do | We don't |
|---|---|
| General lessons on a regular schedule | Answer "what should *I* buy?" for one person |
| A calculator that does math on numbers the user picks | Recommend a stock, fund, coin, broker or app |
| Hypotheticals labeled with their assumptions | Promise or predict returns |
| Show risk next to every return | Show only good periods or testimonials about returns |
| Cite official sources with dates | Use statistics we can't source |

**Before any money changes hands with a financial company:**
- **Brokerage or robo-advisor referrals.** Under the SEC Marketing Rule, an adviser that pays you for referrals must disclose that you're paid, and needs a written agreement above $1,000 a year. The SEC's December 2025 risk alert flagged weak disclosures from influencers and "refer-a-friend" programs. Expect partners to review your content.
- **Never take pay tied to trades or account size** without legal sign-off. Transaction-based pay can look like acting as an unregistered broker.
- **Promoting a specific security for pay** requires disclosing the payment (Securities Act §17(b), the "anti-touting" rule). Simplest policy: never do it.
- **FTC endorsement rules:** any paid relationship gets a clear "#ad" or "Paid partnership" label, right next to the mention.
- **Broker-dealer partners** treat paid creator content as their own communications under FINRA Rule 2210, so they'll want approval rights.
- **Email:** a privacy policy and a working unsubscribe link (CAN-SPAM). Adults 18+ only, so don't market to teens.
- **A personal AI chat advisor** ("ask me what to invest in") is the line not to cross without registering. Keep the AI behind the scenes, writing reviewed, impersonal content.

## Revenue path

| Stage | When | What | Money |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Audience | Months 0–3 | Calculator, weekly lessons, 3–5 short videos a week, newsletter | $0. Goal: 1,000 email subscribers |
| 2. Sponsors | Months 3–6 | Newsletter and video sponsorships from *non-securities* brands (budgeting apps, savings accounts, credit-builder tools), clearly labeled | Per-send or per-video fees |
| 3. Disclosed partnerships | After an attorney review | Flat-fee referral deals with registered advisers or brokers, with SEC Marketing Rule disclosures | Per sign-up, never per trade |
| 4. Paid product | Months 6–12 | "Your first $1,000" self-paced course or cohort ($29–49), still impersonal education | One-time sales |
| 5. **B2B licensing (the big one)** | Months 9+ | White-label the calculator and lessons for **credit unions** (they want young members), **colleges** (first-year financial literacy) and **employers with young workforces** (retail, hospitality, gig) | Annual license or per-seat |

Why B2B is the real business: an institution pays for trusted, compliant content without your needing to sell anything to 20-year-olds. The consumer audience is your proof and your portfolio.

## The first 30 days

| Week | Do | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Trademark search for the name; buy the domain; publish the site (Cloudflare Pages); connect email; set an API spend limit | Site live on your domain, sign-up form works |
| 1–2 | **15–20 interviews** with 18–25 year olds (script below). Friends of friends, campus groups, Discord/Reddit communities you're part of | Notes in one doc, top 3 themes |
| 2 | Turn on the AI team; approve the first lessons and the first social pack | 3+ lessons live |
| 2–4 | Post 3–5 short videos a week from the social packs; share the calculator with real numbers | 12+ videos posted |
| 3 | Optional: $100–200 test of short-video ads pointing at the calculator | Cost per email sign-up known |
| 4 | **Decision gate** (below) | Continue, change the message, or stop |

**Decision gate after 30 days**
- **Keep going** if: 300+ email sign-ups, or 5%+ of calculator visitors sign up, *and* interviews show people want a trusted, no-hype explainer.
- **Change the message** if: lots of visits but few sign-ups (test new hooks and headlines), or interviews show the real blocker is debt or cash, not knowledge (then lead with "before you invest").
- **Stop or pivot** if: little interest after 12+ videos and 20 interviews.

**Numbers to watch weekly:** calculator users, share-link clicks, email sign-up rate, newsletter open rate, follower growth per video, and which hooks get watched past 3 seconds.

**Rough monthly costs (estimates):** domain about $1/month, hosting and email free tiers $0, AI agents roughly $15–45 at 3 tasks a day (set a hard limit in the Claude Console), optional ad test $100–200 once, attorney review one-off.

## Interview script (20 minutes, 18–25 year olds)

Say first: "I'm not selling anything. I'm learning how people your age think about money. There are no wrong answers."

1. What are you doing right now: school, work, both? Do you get a regular paycheck?
2. Walk me through what happens to your money after payday.
3. Do you have any money invested right now? How did that start, or what's stopped you?
4. Where do you learn about money? Which accounts, people or videos do you trust, and why?
5. Have you ever seen investing advice online that turned out to be hype or a scam? What happened?
6. What does "risk" mean to you when it comes to investing?
7. If the market dropped 35% right after you started, what would you do?
8. Do you carry a credit card balance, BNPL payments or student loans? How do you think about those vs. investing?
9. If your employer offered a 401(k) match, would you know what to do with it?
10. *(Show the calculator.)* Pick your own numbers. What surprises you? What's confusing?
11. Would you share a result like this with a friend? Where?
12. What would make you trust a site like this more? Less?
13. Would you rather learn from short videos, a weekly email, an app, or something else?
14. Have you ever paid for money education (a course, app or coaching)? Would you?
15. Who else should I talk to?

Listen for: the exact words people use (use them in hooks), what actually stops them (fear, no money, confusion, debt), and who they trust.
