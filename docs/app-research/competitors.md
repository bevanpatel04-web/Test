# Competitors: who teaches money to young adults, and how they get paid

*Research note for the founder. Checked 2026-11-15. Internal, not consumer copy.*

**How to read this.**
- **Official** = the company's own price page, help center or SEC filing, read on 2026-11-15 unless another date is shown.
- **Vendor-reported** = a number the company says about itself (users, savings, revenue) that nobody independent has checked.
- **Third-party** = a review site, data aggregator or press summary. Treated as weaker.
- **Unverified** = I could not confirm it in a primary source. Don't price or plan off these alone.

## 1. Free, funded by banks or brokers

**Zogo.** Banks, credit unions and fintechs license the platform. They also fund the gift cards users earn for finishing modules ([PYMNTS profile](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=850833); [Zogo home page](https://zogo.com), which pitches to institutions: "Build your customers trust"). Older press release: 200+ institutions and nearly 700,000 users, "the majority of which are under age 24" ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/gen-z-targeted-zogo-app-reaches-milestone-signing-200-financial-institution-partners-301571996.html), undated in my results). A third-party profile cites "more than two million users" as of May 2026 ([YesPress](https://yespress.io/zogo), vendor-reported, **unverified**).
- *Lesson:* Paying people to learn gets installs. The sponsor is a bank that wants accounts. Users don't pay, and they are the product.

**Broker learning centers.** [Robinhood Learn](https://robinhood.com/us/en/learn/) and [Fidelity Learning Center](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/overview-feed) are free. They're funded by the brokerage. Robinhood reported record Q3 2026 revenue of about $1.42B and 5.2M Gold subscribers ([Robinhood Q3 2026 release, via Finviz](https://finviz.com/news/409270/robinhood-reports-third-quarter-2026-results), 2026-10-27). Robinhood also says it will reach 1 million students with classroom financial education by 2030 ([Robinhood newsroom](https://robinhood.com/newsroom/robinhood-to-reach-1-million-with-classroom-based-financial-education-by-2030/), 2026-04-15).
- *Lesson:* The content is decent and free. It's organized around products (options, crypto, margin), not around a beginner's next step.

**Where young people actually learn.** In a 2023 FINRA Foundation and CFA Institute survey, Gen Z investors in the US learned about investing mainly from social media (48%), internet search (47%) and family (45%). Only 30% learned from a financial professional ([FINRA release](https://www.finra.org/media-center/newsreleases/2023/finra-foundation-cfa-institute-research-focuses-gen-z-investors), 2023-05-24; [report PDF](https://www.finrafoundation.org/sites/finrafoundation/files/Gen-Z-and-Investing.pdf)). That is independent research, though now 3 years old.

## 2. Paid apps and courses

**Finimize Pro:** $200 a year (about $17 a month) with a 7-day trial, or about $29 a month. "Pricing varies slightly depending on where you live" ([Finimize help center](https://intercom.help/finimize-help/en/articles/11644196-what-does-finimize-pro-cost), read 2026-11-15). It's market news and research for people who already invest. Finimize also sells to financial firms ("Finimize for Business", 60+ partners per its help center) and says it has a "community of 1.1 million retail investors" ([Finimize community page](https://webflow-business.finimize.com/why-finimize/our-community), vendor-reported).
- *Lesson:* People pay $200/yr for ongoing, fresh content. Our curriculum is finite, so it has to earn its renewal another way (Money Map, review, simulator).

**YNAB:** $14.99 a month or $109 a year. 34-day trial, and a free 365-day trial for college students ([YNAB pricing](https://www.ynab.com/pricing), read 2026-11-15). The page claims new users save "$600 their first month, and more than $6,000 their first year" (vendor-reported, method not shown).
- *Lesson:* A tool people use every week supports a $109 price. A free year for students is a proven way in for our age group.

**Bloom:** $15 a month or $99.99 a year. Includes a brokerage account (custodial for teens 13-17), 200+ lessons and stock rewards. It says subscription fees are how it makes money, not trades or data ([Bloom FAQ](https://bloomapp.com/faq), read 2026-11-15). Its CEO said on a podcast that Bloom has over a million users and "over $2 million a year", growing through TikTok storytelling and micro-influencers ([Superwall podcast](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2522779/episodes/17599372-jae-hwang-this-app-makes-2m-year-with-this-viral-tiktok-strategy), vendor-reported, **unverified**, date not shown).
- *Lesson:* The closest analog to "paid by you." Short-video marketing can carry a money-learning app. Bloom still bundles a brokerage, which we never will.

**Ramsey Financial Peace University:** Ramsey's pages list FPU at $99.99 and "Financial Peace All Access" at $129/yr. Its help center says Ramsey+ "is no longer available for purchase" ([Ramsey pricing](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/ramseyplus/financial-peace/pricing); [help center](https://plus.help.ramseysolutions.com/hc/en-us/articles/19744975004045-What-is-the-difference-between-the-Ramsey-subscription-and-the-Financial-Peace-University-membership); exact current prices **unverified**).
- *Lesson:* A one-time price for a whole path is a real alternative to a subscription.

**Brilliant (pricing analog):** The subscribe page shows $30/month individual and $40/month family ([brilliant.org/subscribe](https://brilliant.org/subscribe/), read 2026-11-15). The annual price wasn't shown. Third-party sites say $149.88 to $240 a year (**unverified**).

**Duolingo (format analog):** Q3 2026: 64.9M daily active users (up 29%), 153.1M monthly active users, 13.1M paid subscribers (up 14%), revenue $309.0M. It is ending its top "Max" tier and folding AI video calls into Super ([shareholder letter, SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026069474/q3fy26duolingo9-30x26share.htm), 2026-11-03). That's about **8.6% of monthly users paying**, after years of tuning. I found no Duolingo money or finance course as of 2026-11-15 (absence not proven). Super prices are about $12.99/mo or $84/yr (third-party, **unverified**).
- *Lesson:* Streaks, short lessons and spaced review are the proven format. A Duolingo finance course would be our biggest threat. Watch for it.

**Coursera (merged with Udemy) and MasterClass.** Coursera Plus lists $399/yr, with a frequent $239 promo ([Coursera Plus](https://coursera.org/courseraplus)). Robert Shiller's Yale course "Financial Markets" shows about 2.4M enrolled and is free to audit ([course page](https://www.coursera.org/learn/financial-markets-global)). For Q3 2026, Coursera's consumer revenue was $175.8M reported but **down about 9% normalized** for the Udemy deal. Management expects consumer revenue to keep slowing ([press summary of Q3 2026 results](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/transcript-coursera-q3-earnings-call-highlights-2026-10-29/), 2026-10-29; I did not read the filing itself). MasterClass is about $120-$240/yr, billed annually (third-party, **unverified**).
- *Lesson:* There's plenty of video finance teaching. Paid consumer demand for generic courses is shrinking.

**Creator courses.** One-time prices are high, e.g. Ramit Sethi's Earnable at $1,499 ([IWT pricing article](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/how-to-price-your-online-course/), updated Nov 2024, may be stale). Graham Stephan's courses are $397-$497 ([third-party](https://ark7.com/blog/?p=30682), **unverified**). These sell on personality and on earning more, not on a structured beginner path. Investopedia Academy sells single courses for around $199 ([third-party review](https://investormint.com/investing/investopedia-academy-review), **unverified**).

## 3. AI-tutor and AI-coach money apps

**Cleo:** Plus $5.99, Pro $8.99 ("advanced AI coaching", savings) and Builder $14.99 (credit-builder card, early paycheck) per month. All tiers include cash advances, up to $250 or $500 ([Cleo pricing](https://web.meetcleo.com/products/pricing), read 2026-11-15). Sacra estimates 2025 revenue at $254M from subscriptions, express fees, interchange and marketplace commissions ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/cleo), aggregator estimate, **unverified**).
- *Lesson:* The largest "AI money coach" earns from lending-adjacent products to people short on cash. That is exactly the conflict our positioning names.

**Origin:** It markets itself as "the first AI financial advisor regulated by the SEC" through Origin Investment Advisory LLC, at about $12.99/mo or $99/yr ([Origin blog](https://useorigin.com/resources/blog/introducing-the-first-sec-regulated-ai-financial-advisor); [press release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250909759834/en/Origin-Unveils-First-AI-Financial-Advisor-Regulated-by-the-SEC-Outsmarts-Every-Leading-AI-Model-on-the-CFP-Exam), 2025-09-09; vendor claims). Another startup, Era, has registered as an adviser that delivers advice through AI ([InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/transformation/no-advisors-no-problem-ai-startup-era-files-as-ria/265785)).
- *Lesson:* Companies whose AI gives personal money answers are choosing to register as advisers. See `rules.md` section 1.

**Small AI tutor apps.** Financebro: AI Finance Teacher charges $6.99/week, $14.99/month or $59.99/year. Its App Store page "hasn't received enough ratings or reviews to display an overview" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6769393561), prices via search summary, **unverified**). EdWealth's "Ed" launched at $39.99/mo or $299.99/yr ([FF News](https://ffnews.com/news/edwealth-debuts-ed-the-ai-financial-coach-aiming-to-level-the-playing-field-for-the-middle-class), 2026-07-16). Khan Academy's tutor Khanmigo is about $4/mo or $44/yr for learners, and Khan's finance content is free ([third-party](https://toolradar.com/tools/khanmigo/pricing), **unverified**).
- *Lesson:* "AI finance tutor" is crowded and cheap to copy, and free general chatbots answer the same questions. The tutor can't be the reason people pay.

## Where the open gap is

**Plainly:** there is a gap in *what the product is*. Demand for it is unproven.

What nobody I found combines:
1. **Paid only by the member, with no product to sell.** Bloom comes closest, but it bundles a brokerage. Finimize also earns from financial firms.
2. **A structured beginner-to-advanced path with mastery checks and spaced review.** Duolingo has the format, but not for money. Ramsey has a path, but one strong ideology and no adaptive review.
3. **Behavior practice.** I found no consumer product built around living through historical crashes with a hypothetical portfolio. Broker sites have calculators, not this.
4. **Short video made for both learning and TikTok.**

Why the gap may stay small:
- **Free is good enough for many.** Gen Z already learns from social media and search. Shiller's course is free to audit, and chatbots answer for free.
- **Courses end.** A finite curriculum invites cancellation once it's finished. Coursera's consumer business is shrinking.
- **Our price is already being tested.** Financebro is at $59.99/yr and shows no visible traction yet. Bloom's ~$100/yr reportedly works at roughly $2M a year (**unverified**), with a brokerage attached.

## So what for us

1. **Sell structure and behavior, not information.** "Five levels, a crash simulator, your Money Map" is defensible. "Videos about money" and "an AI tutor" are not.
2. **Lead with the honesty contrast, carefully.** Zogo, Cleo and broker learning centers are all funded by selling financial products, which makes "Paid by you, not by banks" a true and distinctive line. Never name or criticize competitors in consumer copy.
3. **Price like an app, not a course.** The relevant peers sit at $60-$110 a year (Financebro, Duolingo, Bloom, YNAB). Finimize's $200 is for active investors. See `pricing-and-retention.md` for the recommendation.
4. **Give members a reason to stay after Level 5.** That means the Money Map, yearly "re-run your numbers" check-ins and new simulator scenarios. Otherwise expect cancellations at completion.
5. **Copy Bloom's distribution, not its model.** Short video plus micro-creators is how a Gen Z money app grew, by its own account.
6. **Say no to B2B money from financial firms.** Finimize and Zogo both take it, and it would quietly break our positioning.
7. **Watch two threats:** a Duolingo money course, and free chatbots getting better at structured teaching.
