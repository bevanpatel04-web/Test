# Regulatory checklist: questions for an attorney

**NOT LEGAL ADVICE. DRAFT for attorney review.** This is a research list of questions, with what I found, so an attorney's hour is used well. Laws vary by state and change often. As of 2026-11-14.

**Pilot as designed (from `docs/pilot/BRIEF.md`):** the employer pays a PEPM fee and funds a reward pool. Members aged 18-25 earn points for tenure milestones (30/90/180/365 days), lessons and money habits. They redeem for a **payroll bonus** (paid by the employer through payroll) or a **gift card**. We move no money and offer no securities in the pilot.

## Top 5 things to raise first

1. **Age-limited eligibility (18-25) may be age discrimination.** Federal law protects workers 40 and over. Some states also protect younger workers.
2. **Every reward is taxable wages**, including gift cards and any points the employer pays for. Gift cards must go through payroll too.
3. **Tenure rewards for hourly workers count toward overtime pay** (the federal "regular rate" rule).
4. **Unredeemed points may be treated as unpaid wages** when someone quits, with final-pay and unclaimed-property consequences.
5. **The later "points into investing" phase** brings broker-dealer, promoter and gamification rules. "Free stock rewards" were named in Massachusetts' 2024 Robinhood order.

## A. Tax treatment

**What I found**
- **Cash and cash equivalents are never a tax-free "de minimis" fringe.** IRS: "Cash or cash equivalent items provided by the employer are never excludable from income… Gift certificates that are redeemable for general merchandise or have a cash equivalent value are not de minimis benefits and are taxable" ([IRS, De minimis fringe benefits](https://www.irs.gov/government-entities/federal-state-local-governments/de-minimis-fringe-benefits)). Same position in [Technical Advice Memorandum 200437030](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-wd/0437030.pdf).
- **The "length of service award" exclusion doesn't help.** It covers only tangible personal property. It "doesn't apply to awards of cash, cash equivalents, gift cards, gift coupons, or gift certificates" or to "stocks, bonds, other securities" ([IRS Publication 15-B (2026)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15b); added by the 2017 tax law, [Pub. L. 115-97](https://www.congress.gov/115/plaws/publ97/PLAW-115publ97.pdf)). I believe length-of-service awards also can't qualify in an employee's first 5 years, which would rule out 30-365 day milestones anyway (**unverified**: not confirmed in the 2026 text I read).
- **Withholding:** bonuses are "supplemental wages." Employers may withhold federal income tax at a flat 22% ([IRS Publication 15 (2026)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p15.pdf)), plus Social Security and Medicare.
- **401(k) incentives are a special case.** SECURE 2.0 lets employers give a "de minimis financial incentive" of no more than $250 to encourage 401(k) participation, and it is taxable ([IRS Notice 2024-2](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-24-02.pdf)). Otherwise, a benefit conditioned on making 401(k) deferrals is generally not allowed.

**Questions**
1. Are points taxable when **earned** or when **redeemed**? If points can always become cash or a gift card, does "constructive receipt" make them taxable on award?
2. When an employee picks a gift card, how does the employer report and withhold? (The value must go on the W-2 and tax is withheld from regular pay.) What data must we send the employer, and how often?
3. Who is the payer of wages when we order a gift card that the employer paid for? Any risk that **we** are treated as a third-party payer with reporting duties?
4. If we ever reward "enrolling in the 401(k)," do we need to cap it at $250 and keep it out of plan assets? Should we avoid that rule entirely?
5. Member copy: the data contract says payroll bonus is "taxed like regular pay." Gift cards are taxed too. Can we say "All rewards are taxable pay" everywhere?

## B. Wage-and-hour (federal FLSA and state law)

**What I found**
- **Overtime "regular rate."** Bonuses "announced to employees to induce them to work more steadily or more rapidly or more efficiently **or to remain with the firm** are regarded as part of the regular rate of pay" ([29 CFR 778.211](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-V/subchapter-B/part-778/subpart-C/subject-group-ECFR29a4ab910b4eed8/section-778.211)). Tenure-milestone rewards look like exactly this. For hourly staff who worked overtime during the earning period, the employer may owe extra overtime pay.
- **Lesson time.** Training need not be paid working time only if all four are true: outside regular hours, truly voluntary, not directly related to the job, and no productive work ([29 CFR 785.27](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-V/subchapter-B/part-785/subpart-C/subject-group-ECFR517a5a13c426150/section-785.27)). Money lessons are probably "not job related," but a manager pushing staff to do them on shift could change that.

**Questions**
6. Are tenure, lesson and habit rewards "non-discretionary bonuses" that must go into the regular rate? How should the employer allocate them back across the earning period? Can we produce that report?
7. Can lessons be done on the clock, off the clock, or both? What wording keeps them voluntary?
8. **Forfeiture:** when someone quits or is fired, can unredeemed points expire? Are earned points "wages" that must be paid in the final paycheck in states like California? Should points auto-convert to a payroll bonus at separation?
9. Is a gift card an acceptable way to pay a non-discretionary bonus under state wage-payment laws, or must wages be paid in cash/check/direct deposit?
10. In unionized hotels or restaurants, does a new reward program have to be bargained with the union?

## C. Eligibility and discrimination

**What I found**
- The federal Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA) protects people **40 or older**, including in compensation and benefits ([EEOC, Age discrimination](https://www.eeoc.gov/age-discrimination); [29 CFR Part 1625](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-XIV/part-1625)). Favoring older workers is allowed; **excluding workers 40+ from a reward offered to younger workers appears to be the problem case.**
- EEOC also notes "some states have laws that protect younger workers." New Jersey protects workers 18 and older; New York state and city law reach younger workers ([AARP summary](https://www.aarp.org/work/working-at-50-plus/info-2021/millennials-age-discrimination.html), secondary; statutes not checked, **unverified**).

**Questions**
11. Is an employer-paid reward limited to employees aged 18-25 lawful under the ADEA and state law? (My read: risky.)
12. If we open it to **all hourly employees** but design content for young workers, is that clean?
13. Can we exclude employees under 18 (common in restaurants) for privacy and contract reasons?
14. Does any reward rule have a disparate impact (for example, rewards only for direct deposit to a savings account, which unbanked workers can't do)?

## D. Gift cards, points and unclaimed property (escheat)

**What I found**
- Federal gift card rules (Credit CARD Act, Regulation E) **exclude "loyalty, award, or promotional" gift cards** from most requirements, such as the 5-year expiration minimum, but still require certain disclosures ([12 CFR 1005.20](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1005/subpart-A/section-1005.20); [CFPB commentary](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1005/20/)).
- Unclaimed property: the 2016 Uniform Unclaimed Property Act excludes loyalty cards given without money paid; states that updated since 2016 generally exempt them ([Alston & Bird cheat sheet](https://www.alston.com/files/docs/UPLCheatSheet2023Edition/index.html), [Baker Donelson](https://www.bakerdonelson.com/Loyalty-Reward-Programs-Raise-a-Host-of-Questions-under-State-Unclaimed-Property-Laws-06-30-2011)). But **unclaimed wages** escheat after about one year under the Uniform Act ([JD Supra summary](https://production.jdsupra.com/legalnews/unclaimed-property-what-s-new-for-gift-66136), secondary). Arizona's SB 1336 reportedly repealed some gift card and loyalty exemptions ([Alston & Bird, Aug 2026](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/repeal-arizona-unclaimed-property-exemptions); **unverified** bill status).

**Questions**
15. Are our points "loyalty/award" value (often exempt) or **earned compensation** (unclaimed wages)? Who is the holder: us, the employer, or the gift card vendor?
16. If an employer prefunds a pool and some is never redeemed, who keeps it, and is it reportable? (Simplest: no prefunding; the employer pays only when a reward is redeemed.)
17. What disclosures must appear on an award gift card or its email (expiry, fees)?

## E. Sweepstakes and lottery rules

**What I found**
- A promotion is generally an illegal lottery if it has a **prize, chance and consideration**. Remove any one and it isn't ([Holland & Knight, 2022](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2022/05/marketers-beware-your-social-media-sweepstakes-or-contests-could-be)). "Consideration" can be read broadly, including effort, and some states (e.g., Wisconsin) treat mostly-chance outcomes as lotteries even with some skill ([Wisconsin DFI guidance](https://charitable.wi.gov/Content/PDF/ContestsSweepstakesPromotions%204.15.pdf)).

**Questions**
18. Confirm that fixed, earned rewards with no random element are outside lottery and sweepstakes law.
19. If marketing ever wants a "spin to win," prize draw or mystery bonus: is working a shift or doing a lesson "consideration"? (Recommendation: never add chance. It also keeps us away from gamification concerns.)

## F. Employee data privacy

**What I found**
- **California:** since 2023-01-01, employee and HR data is covered by the CCPA/CPRA. Employees get access, correction and deletion rights; employers need an employee privacy notice and must sign specific contract terms with vendors (service providers) ([Holland & Knight, 2022-12-20](https://hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2022/12/california-employee-data-exemption-expires-on-january-1)).
- Most other state comprehensive privacy laws (e.g., Colorado, Connecticut, Utah, Virginia) exclude data collected in an employment context ([SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/in/topics-tools/employment-law-compliance/california-employers-get-ready-expanded-privacy-compliance-begins-2023)). Not checked state by state (**unverified** for newer laws such as Texas).
- Our own data contract shows the employer each member's points, lessons completed and last activity. Financial-habit data (who set up savings) is sensitive, even if not legally "sensitive."

**Questions**
20. What is the minimum data the employer may share with us to run tenure rewards (employee ID, hire date, active/terminated, location, email or phone)? Do we need date of birth? (Not if eligibility is open to all ages.)
21. Do we need a service-provider agreement with each employer (CCPA) and a privacy notice for California workers?
22. Should the employer see only **aggregates** for lessons and money habits, never per-person data? Could per-person visibility create a claim that the employer used financial information against a worker?
23. Texting and email: what consent is needed under TCPA and CAN-SPAM for reward reminders?

## G. Money transmission

**What I found**
- **Payroll bonus:** the employer pays its own employee through its own payroll. We never hold or move the money. That looks like the safest path.
- **Gift cards (federal):** FinCEN's prepaid-access rules exclude closed-loop gift cards up to $2,000 per card per day, and arrangements giving access solely to **"employment benefits, incentives, wages or salaries"** if they don't allow international transfers, transfers between users, or reloading from non-bank sources ([31 CFR 1010.100(ff)(4)(iii)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.100)).
- **States:** 31 states have adopted the Money Transmission Modernization Act in full or part as of 2026-09-03 ([CSBS](https://www.csbs.org/node/231461)). The model act **includes "payroll processing services" in money transmission**, and states have split on exemptions (e.g., Nevada removed it, [AB 430](https://archive.leg.state.nv.us/Session/83rd2025/Bills/AB/AB430_EN.pdf); Delaware notice to payroll processors, [Aug 2026](https://banking.delaware.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/73/2026/08/Payroll-Processor-Notice-Aug-21-2026.pdf)).

**Questions**
24. If we buy gift cards from a vendor (Tremendous, Tango) using money the employer sent us, are we selling stored value or transmitting money in any state? Is it cleaner for the **employer to hold the vendor account** and for us to send only instructions?
25. Are open-loop prepaid cards (e.g., Visa reward cards) riskier than closed-loop store cards for us?
26. If we ever calculate or deliver payroll bonuses on the employer's behalf, could that be "payroll processing services" under MTMA states?

## H. Later phase: points into the member's own investing account

**What I found**
- **Brokers and finders:** anyone paid per transaction for bringing in securities business may need to register as a broker. FINRA Rule 2040 bars member firms from paying transaction-based compensation to people who would have to register ([FINRA Rule 2040](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/2040); [Regulatory Notice 15-07](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/15-07)).
- **SEC Marketing Rule (if the partner is an investment adviser):** paid promoters need disclosures; a written agreement is required unless pay is $1,000 or less over 12 months ([17 CFR 275.206(4)-1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/275.206(4)-1); [SEC final rule](https://www.sec.gov/rules/final/2020/ia-5653.pdf)). The SEC's 2025-12-16 risk alert flagged weak disclosure of compensation, including referral payments and influencer content ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/whats-new/additional-observations-regarding-advisers-compliance-advisers-act-marketing-rule)).
- **FINRA communications:** a broker-dealer partner treats content it pays for or adopts as its own communications under Rule 2210, so it will want to review ours (see `docs/patient-money-plan.md`).
- **Gamification:** Massachusetts fined Robinhood $7.5M on 2024-01-18. The order cited **confetti animations, digital scratch tickets, free stock rewards**, push notifications and "most popular" lists ([ThinkAdvisor](https://www.thinkadvisor.com/2024/01/18/robinhood-to-pay-7-5m-over-gamification-practices/), [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/regulation-and-legislation/news/robinhood-to-pay-7-5m-to-settle-massachusetts-charges-248214)). Massachusetts' fiduciary rule was upheld by the state's highest court in Aug 2023 (per the same coverage). At the federal level the SEC withdrew its "predictive data analytics" proposal on 2025-06-12 ([Alston & Bird](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2025/06/sec-withdraws-multiple-rule-proposals)), so state regulators and FINRA are now the main watchdogs.
- **Securities Act §17(b)** (anti-touting): paid promotion of a specific security requires disclosure. Our rule: never promote a security.

**Questions**
27. If an employer pays us a flat PEPM and a broker partner pays us nothing per account or trade, are we clear of broker registration? What if the partner pays per funded account?
28. Is a stock-rewards deposit (e.g., Apex or Bits of Stock) an employer "sale" or "gift" of securities to employees? Any registration or disclosure duty for the employer?
29. Does an employer depositing money into employees' own investing accounts risk being an ERISA plan?
30. Partnering with **one** broker effectively picks a broker for members. Does that conflict with our "never recommend a broker or app" rule? Would "route your payroll bonus by direct deposit to any account you choose" avoid both issues?
31. Which state regulators (Massachusetts first) would see milestone rewards that end in an investing account as gamification, even if rewards never depend on trading?
32. What FINRA 2210 review process should we agree with a partner before launch?

## So what for us

- **Change eligibility now** to "all hourly employees 18+" (pending attorney sign-off), and keep the 18-25 focus in content and marketing only. This is the cheapest fix with the biggest legal payoff.
- **Treat every reward as wages run through the employer's payroll**, gift cards included. Tell employers up front about the overtime regular-rate rule. Have the backend export a per-pay-period report the employer's payroll can use.
- **Don't prefund or hold reward money.** The employer pays only when a reward is redeemed, ideally through its own gift-card vendor account. That avoids most money-transmission and escheat questions.
- **Decide what happens to points when someone leaves** (likely: auto-pay as a final bonus) before launch.
- **Show employers aggregates only** for lessons and money habits.
- **Never add chance** (no draws, spins or mystery prizes).
- **For phase 2, test "route your bonus to your own account" before any broker partnership.** It needs no securities partner and doesn't pick a broker.
- Budget for **two** attorney reviews: an employment/tax lawyer for the pilot, and a securities lawyer before phase 2.
