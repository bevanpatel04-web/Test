# Competitors: who already sells to employers of young hourly workers

*Research note. As of 2026-11-14. Internal, not consumer-facing.*

**How to read this.** "Vendor claim" means the company said it about itself, or paid for the study. "Independent" means someone with no stake in the result produced it. **Unverified** means I could not confirm it in a primary source. Funding and valuation numbers are often from data aggregators (Tracxn, PitchBook, Sacra) and are labeled that way.

## 1. Earned wage access (EWA)

EWA lets a worker take pay they have already earned before payday. It is the financial benefit most likely to already be in our target employers.

**DailyPay**
- What: on-demand pay inside the employer's payroll.
- Who pays: free to employers; workers pay for instant transfers ($3.49 on the small-business page, ranging $1.99-$3.99 by employer). Next-day transfers are free. Sources: [Workinsiders](https://workinsiders.com/tools/dailypay/), [HR.software](https://www.hr.software/reviews/dailypay) (third-party, **unverified**).
- Status: valuation about $1.75B as of 2026-01-01, down 42% from a $3B peak in May 2021; $200M debt round Feb 2026 ([Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/dailypay/__u3PClVnK8iDghH-J3PYieVzKtXF18kBOmctL0rVpgIg), aggregator).
- Retention claim: "30% of employers say they've seen a reduction in turnover", from a survey DailyPay **commissioned** from Arizent ([DailyPay press release](https://www.dailypay.com/press-center/press-releases/dailypay-improves-employee-retention-productivity-and-morale-according-to/)). Self-reported, no comparison group.

**Payactiv**
- What: EWA, plus a marketplace in the app.
- Who pays: offered to employers at no cost ([Payactiv](https://payactiv.com/)). Reported to have eliminated access fees on several delivery options, including ACH ([FF News](https://ffnews.com/newsarticle/payactiv-transforms-earned-wage-access-by-eliminating-access-fees/), date **unverified**).
- Recent moves (per [Payactiv press page](https://payactiv.com/press/), dates as listed there): UKG Frontline Worker Network (2026-10-08); **Blackhawk Network digital gift cards in the Payactiv app**, announced Oct 2026, launching Nov 2026 ([release via Webull](https://www.webull.com/news/15780704857662464)); Oak View Group venues (2026-10-22).
- Retention claim: a 2018 Harvard Kennedy School paper by Todd Baker and Snigdha Kumar found **19% lower turnover among active users**, using about 6,700 records from 6 companies enrolled in Payactiv ([summary hosted by Payactiv](https://www.payactiv.com//assets/pdf/4-big-benefits-MRCBG-paper-20181004.pdf)). This compares users with non-users, so people who planned to stay may simply be the ones who signed up. Payactiv marketing elsewhere says "up to 30%."

**Branch**
- What: EWA plus a worker wallet and debit card. In 2026 launched "Flex" EWA, including a version banks and credit unions brand as their own ([Branch](https://branchapp.com/blog/flex-embedded-ewa), [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/fintech/news/branch-launches-flex-ewa-model-for-banks-and-credit-unions)).
- Who pays: Branch says fee-free for workers and employers (company claim, July 2026). Revenue reportedly comes from debit-card interchange (older article, **unverified**).

**Regulatory backdrop:** on 2025-12-23 the CFPB issued an advisory opinion that employer-partnered ("covered") EWA is not credit under the Truth in Lending Act ([CFPB PDF](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_earned-wage-access-advisory-opinion_2025-12.pdf)). States can still regulate it. This lowers the legal risk for EWA providers and makes them stronger in employer sales.

**What we learn:** EWA is already sold to the same restaurants and retailers, and it is "free" to the employer. The employer's HR buyer will ask why they should pay $4 per employee when Payactiv is $0. EWA providers already sit in payroll and are adding gift cards (Payactiv plus Blackhawk). They could add tenure rewards in a quarter. We are a complement to EWA, not a substitute.

## 2. Financial wellness

**Brightside**: financial coaching and products for employees. Employer-paid, custom pricing not disclosed ([SelectSoftware Reviews](https://www.selectsoftwarereviews.com/reviews/brightside)). Markets a "41% reduction in turnover" and cites a WTW figure that poor financial health costs large employers $3,000-$4,000 per employee per year ([Brightside](https://gobrightside.com)). Both are **vendor claims**.

**Origin (useorigin.com)**: started as an employer financial-planning benefit, then added a consumer subscription at $12.99/month or $99/year and an "AI Financial Advisor" it says is SEC-regulated (Sept 2025) ([Origin blog](https://useorigin.com/resources/blog/origin-fall-launch), [BusinessWire, 2025-09-09](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250909759834/en/Origin-Unveils-First-AI-Financial-Advisor-Regulated-by-the-SEC-Outsmarts-Every-Leading-AI-Model-on-the-CFP-Exam)). Caution: a *different* company also called Origin (benefits analytics) raised $30M in March 2026 ([Fortune, 2026-03-25](https://fortune.com/2026/03/25/exclusive-ai-powered-benefits-platform-origin-raises-30-million-in-fresh-funding-to-bring-chros-visibility-into-benefits-usage-and-spend/)). Don't mix them up.

**BrightPlan**: financial wellness for large employers. Series C led by ABS Capital, first close Sept 2026, size undisclosed ([FinTech Global, 2026-09-09](https://fintech.global/2026/09/09/brightplan-lands-series-c-led-by-abs-capital-partners/)). Claims 10M+ employees served and 0% enterprise churn (**vendor claims**). Pricing not public.

**SmartDollar (Ramsey Solutions)**: employer-paid, free to staff. Pricing is custom: an annual fee by number of eligible employees, a utilization-based model, or a **pilot program** ([Ramsey consultant page](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/corporate-wellness/financial-wellness/consultant)). Claims 2,000 businesses and 3.5M employees served ([Ramsey](https://ramseysolutions.com/corporate-wellness/financial-wellness/financial-wellness-benefit), **vendor claim**).

**Northstar**: financial planning benefit. Raised about $40M; last round $24.4M in Oct 2022 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/10/13/fintech-startup-raises-24m-to-be-employees-northstar-when-it-comes-to-financial-advice)). **Acquired by Nayya**, announced 2025-09-10 ([Nayya](https://www.nayya.com/blog/nayya-acquires-northstar-and-unveils-first-of-its-kind-agentic-ai-health-and-wealth-benefits-solution)). One aggregator lists the employer price at $8 per employee per month ([sp-edge](https://sp-edge.com/companies/926949), **unverified**, undated).

**What we learn:** this category sells to HR at large employers, mostly salaried staff. Prices are rarely public. Where a number exists ($8 PEPM for Northstar, **unverified**), our $4 is in range. The category is consolidating (Nayya bought Northstar) and moving to AI advice. That is a line we must not cross: our rules say no personal advice.

## 3. Emergency savings

**Sunny Day Fund**: employees save by payroll deduction into an account at Portage Bank (Member FDIC). **Employers or foundations fund cash rewards for saving regularly.** Reported results ([Financial Health Network, 2026-02-02](https://finhealthnetwork.org/sunny-day-fund-helping-the-entire-workplace-thrive-with-emergency-savings/), data supplied by Sunny Day Fund):
- 40% participation at one large employer.
- Savers put in about $211 a month on average in 2025.
- Turnover of 5.7% among savers vs 25.2% in the broader eligible workforce.

That last number is a **vendor claim with an obvious selection problem**: steady workers are the ones who sign up to save. Partners include FinFit ([PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/finfit-partners-with-sunny-day-fund-to-add-emergency-savings-accounts-to-its-financial-wellness-platform-302250110.html)). Pricing not public.

**SecureSave**: similar product. Its survey says 3 in 4 workers would be more likely to stay if the employer put in $200 a year ([SecureSave](https://www.securesave.com/blog/research-financial-stress-emergency-savings-and-the-impact-on-american-workers), **vendor claim**, stated intention not behavior). A Commonwealth case study (supported by BlackRock's Emergency Savings Initiative) reports 73% enrollment after one year at a healthcare employer with 50,000+ eligible staff, with no retention comparison group ([Commonwealth, Nov 2025](https://buildcommonwealth.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/CW_Driving-Impact-Case-Study.pdf)).

**What we learn:** "employer-funded rewards for good money habits" already exists. Sunny Day Fund's headline retention number is exactly the kind of comparison our pilot dashboard will make: enrolled vs not enrolled. It will look great and prove little. See `turnover-economics.md`.

## 4. Recognition and rewards platforms

- **Bonusly**: peer-recognition points, about $3 per user per month on its Team plan ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/167338/Bonusly/pricing/), third-party, **unverified**).
- **Workhuman, Achievers**: enterprise recognition, custom pricing. Achievers markets frontline tools such as QR codes, physical cards and kiosks ([Achievers](https://www.achievers.com/blog/achievers-vs-workhuman/), vendor).
- **Bucketlist Rewards**: points from peer and manager recognition **and automated milestone triggers, including 90-day and work-anniversary milestones for retail frontline staff** through a Zipline integration. Employers pay only for points redeemed ([Bucketlist](https://bucketlistrewards.com/blog/frontline-employee-recognition/), vendor).
- **Bilt + Kastle** (announced 2026-11-10): office workers get rewards at nearby businesses as they use building access badges. Pitched to employers to "support attendance, recruitment and retention." Kastle's systems are in 60,000 businesses; Bilt has 50,000+ merchant partners ([Facilities Dive, 2026-11-13](https://www.facilitiesdive.com/news/kastle-bilt-deal-creates-employee-workplace-incentives/874641/)). Who pays was not stated.

**What we learn:** "points for hitting 30/90/365 days" is a feature that already exists and is cheap. It can't be our whole product. And Bilt itself is now selling a workplace-retention story, though to office buildings, not restaurants.

## 5. Stock rewards and rewards-into-investing

**Apex Fintech Solutions**: clearing firm with a **Stock Rewards API** that lets brands and banks pay customers in fractional shares ([Apex](https://apexfintechsolutions.com/who-we-serve/consumer-brands/)); patents announced March 2024 ([Apex](https://apexfintechsolutions.com/press-releases/apex-stock-rewards-api-receives-new-patent-approvals/)). Parent is PEAK6. Filed confidentially for an IPO in Dec 2023; not completed as of March 2026 ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/fintech/apex-fintech-solutions-renews-ipo-plans-files-documents-with-the-sec/)). 2026 newsroom items include Navy Federal and Cash App Investing mandates ([Apex newsroom](https://apexfintechsolutions.com/newsroom/), headlines only). Apex marketing cites a survey it **commissioned** (Appinio, May 2025, 2,000 adults): 52% likely to switch brands for stock rewards.

**DriveWealth**: embedded brokerage for apps. 2026 news is mostly international and new products (Ualá in Mexico, TrueMoney, BtcTurk, Kalshi, Ripple) ([DriveWealth 2026](https://www.drivewealth.com/2026/)). Valuation $2.9B as of 2025-12-31 ([Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/drivewealth/__1yqx6no1A8IB1Y4rAkqxfjkEu9nEFxq7z5mJ3dtiY0g), **unverified**). FINRA fined DriveWealth $1.1M and censured it over failures to report fractional-share trades, conduct Feb 2016-Mar 2022 ([FINRA AWC 2020067647701](https://data-portal.finra.org/fda_documents/2020067647701%20Drivewealth%2C%20LLC%20CRD%20165429%20AWC%20lp.pdf); settlement date **unverified**). A Stake data-security incident in Sept 2026 was listed in search results (**unverified**).

**Bits of Stock**: "Growth Rewards" API. Sells stock-rewards checking to banks and credit unions through Jack Henry (announced 2024-01-09) and Q2 ([Nasdaq release](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/stock-rewards-checking-now-accessible-to-financial-institutions-through-bits-of-stock)). Says consumer rewards are "other income" for tax ([FAQ](https://bitsofstock.com/app-support/faq)). Cites an NYU Stern study of spending lift (**vendor-commissioned**; two different figures quoted).

**Grifin**: consumer app that buys stock in brands you shop at, using your own money. $11M Series A on 2025-06-18 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/18/grifin-secures-11-million-to-make-investing-less-intimidating-for-its-female-user-base)).

**Failures**
- **Stockpile** (stock gift cards) **closed 2026-04-17**; accounts moved to Public, Stash or Apex ([Doctor of Credit](https://www.doctorofcredit.com/stockpile-to-close-on-april-17-2026-accounts-transferred-to-public-stash-apex/)).
- **Bumped** (brand-funded stock rewards) ended rewards in July 2022 and wound down that December ([Grifin blog](https://www.grifin.com/post/what-happened-to-bumped-and-is-grifin-the-replacement); Grifin is a competitor, so treat as a secondhand account).

**What we learn:** brand-funded and gift-funded stock rewards have failed twice. The survivors sell to **banks and credit unions** as an engagement tool. That supports "credit unions as second payer." It also means the later investing phase depends on a partner whose economics we don't control.

## 6. Bilt itself

- **Who pays:** landlords pay payment-processing fees, reported at 0.6%-0.9% of rent volume ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/bilt), aggregator, **unverified**). Also merchant referral fees and card interchange.
- **Funding:** $250M at a $10.75B valuation, July 2025, co-led by General Catalyst and GID (widely reported, e.g. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilt_Rewards); not confirmed in a primary release).
- **The subsidy problem:** the WSJ reported Wells Fargo was losing up to $10M a month on the Bilt card ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/proptech/wells-fargo-loses-millions-each-month-from-rent-payment-proptech-partnership-124728)). The partnership ended; Sacra dates the end to 2026-02-06. Bilt Card 2.0 moved to Cardless with tiered annual fees of $0/$95/$495 ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/bilt)).
- **Operations risk:** after the switch, users reported failed rent payments ([Forbes, 2026-03-10](https://www.forbes.com/sites/aliciapark/2026/03/10/no-remorse-no-accountability-bilt-users-in-uproar-over-new-credit-cards/)). The Senate Banking Committee wrote to Bilt on 2026-05-27 about delayed, denied or lost payments ([letter](https://www.banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/2026.05.27%20Letter%20to%20Bilt.pdf)).
- **2027 changes:** new cardholders get housing-only rewards; Bilt Cash expires on a rolling 12 months ([The Points Guy, 2026-10-27](https://thepointsguy.com/news/bilt-changes-2027)). Bilt is cutting reward costs.
- **Workplace move:** the Kastle deal (Nov 2026, above).

**What we learn:** Bilt's payer pays a small slice of money it already moves (rent). For employers, the equivalent is payroll. Bilt also shows two ways it can go wrong: a partner subsidizing rewards at a loss, and payment operations breaking. Our pilot avoids both by moving no money ourselves.

## Where the gap is (and isn't)

**Every piece of our pilot already exists somewhere:**
- tenure-milestone points for frontline staff (Bucketlist),
- employer-funded rewards for saving (Sunny Day Fund),
- financial education as a benefit (SmartDollar, Brightside),
- free money benefits already in payroll (DailyPay, Payactiv, Branch),
- stock rewards for credit-union members (Bits of Stock, Apex).

**What I did not find anywhere:** one product that combines (1) employer-paid tenure rewards, (2) honest, no-advice money education built for 18-25 year olds, and (3) a retention readout an employer can trust, sold to restaurants, hotels and retail. That is a real but **narrow** gap. It can be copied, especially by an EWA provider that already sits in the employer's payroll.

Our possible edges are not features. They are (a) **trust**: education with no product to sell; (b) **measurement**: proving retention honestly, which no one in this list does; and (c) **focus** on young hourly workers in hospitality.

## So what for us

1. **Don't claim to be first** with tenure points or savings rewards. Claim to be the honest, measured, young-worker version.
2. **Position next to EWA, not against it.** Most target employers will already have DailyPay, Payactiv or Branch. An EWA provider could be a distribution partner, and could also become a competitor.
3. **Expect price pushback.** Buyers compare against "free" EWA and ~$3 recognition tools. $4 PEPM is defensible only with a believable retention number.
4. **Our measurement is the product.** Every retention claim above is a users-vs-non-users comparison. If we run a fair test (see `turnover-economics.md`), that alone sets us apart.
5. **Treat the investing phase as optional.** Brand- and gift-funded stock rewards failed (Bumped 2022, Stockpile 2026). Credit unions are the payer that has stuck with stock rewards.
6. **Watch Bilt.** Its Kastle deal shows it wants the workplace. Today that is office buildings, not shift workers.
