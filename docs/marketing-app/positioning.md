# Positioning: the Patient Money learning app

*Owner: marketing. Draft of 2026-11-15, built from `docs/app/BRIEF.md`. For the founder and every agent who writes consumer copy. Prices are DRAFT. Audience: adults 18-30. Never address or target anyone under 18.*

## The short version

**Tagline:** Paid by you, not by banks.

**One-line promise:** Learn money in the right order, from your first paycheck to advanced investing, from a company that earns only from you.

**Three proof points**

1. **A path, not a pile.** Five levels, about 45 short lessons, unlocked in order. Each lesson ends with a 3-question check, and you need 2 of 3 right to complete it. Spaced review brings old ideas back. You always know what's next and what you've actually learned.
2. **Nothing to sell you.** No ads. No affiliate links. No card, loan, brokerage or crypto referrals. Every lesson cites official sources with dates, passes an automatic check for hype and advice-style language, and is approved by a person before it goes live. Our rules are public (`rules.html`).
3. **Practice with your own numbers.** Worksheets build a private Money Map that stays on your device. The crash simulator lets you live through hypothetical market crashes with a pretend index portfolio, so you see what holding or selling would do before it's ever your real money. (Replaying real crashes like 2008-09 needs licensed index data first; see `docs/app-research/rules.md`.)

**What we are not:** an advisor, a broker, a bank or a stock-picking service. We explain how money works; members make their own calls.

## Brand voice guide

**We sound like:** a calm, sharp friend who's good with money, explains things in plain words and has nothing to sell you.

### Five principles

1. **Plain over clever.** Short sentences. Everyday words. Explain any term the first time it shows up ("APR, the yearly interest rate on a loan or card").
2. **Calm over urgent.** No countdowns, no fake scarcity, no "everyone's doing it". A good money decision can wait a day, and so can joining.
3. **Honest over impressive.** Show the risk beside any return. Show bad years, not only good ones. Say "we don't know" when nobody knows.
4. **Respect over preaching.** No shame about debt, spending or starting late. We explain; the member decides.
5. **"How it works" over "what to do".** We describe options and trade-offs ("many people...", "one approach is...", "the trade-off is..."). We never tell a person what to do with their own money.

### Mechanics

- **Reading level:** aim for 8th grade. Read it out loud: if it sounds like a brochure, rewrite it.
- **Person:** "you" and "we". Contractions are fine.
- **Numbers:** digits ("3 lessons", "$100 a month"). Every money outcome uses "could", states its assumptions (amount, rate, years) and says it's hypothetical, not a prediction.
- **Sources:** every statistic shows its source and date on screen and in the caption.
- **Risk line:** any mention of growth or returns sits next to "Investments can lose value, including money you put in" or an equally clear line.
- **Exclamation marks:** none in anything about money. One is fine in a thank-you message.
- **Emoji:** none in lessons. At most one in a social caption, never a money, rocket or fire emoji.
- **Humor:** dry and kind, aimed at the situation or at ourselves, never at someone's money mistakes.
- **Names:** never name or recommend a specific stock, fund, coin, broker, bank or app. Broad categories are fine (index fund, 401(k), high-yield savings account).
- **Age:** for adults 18 and over. Never write for, picture or target teens.

### Words we use and words we avoid

| We say | We avoid | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "could", "in this example", "hypothetical" | predictions about what markets or prices do next | Nobody knows, and the claims filter blocks predictions |
| "many people check first...", "one approach is..." | "you should", "you need to" about money moves | We describe options; members decide |
| "the trade-off is..." | "smart move", "dumb move" | Respect over preaching |
| "Level 1 is free" | "free trial" pressure tricks, countdown timers | Calm over urgent |
| "Made with AI, reviewed by a person" | "expert-made", "certified", "AI-powered advice" | Honest about how it's made |
| "investments can lose value" | any word that makes an investment sound certain | Risk beside every return |

The full list of blocked hype and advice phrases is in `invest/claims.py`. Run every script, caption and page through it. Passing the filter is the floor, not the goal: copy can pass and still sound pushy.

### Rewrites: tone problems the filter won't catch

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| "You're behind if you haven't started investing by 25." | "Starting later is common. Here's what time does, with numbers you pick." |
| "Stop wasting money on coffee." | "A budget is a plan for your money, not a punishment. Coffee can be in the plan." |

| "Join thousands of members." (before it's true) | "Join the waitlist. We'll email you when membership opens." |

### Look and sound

- Use the site's design tokens (`invest/site/assets/style.css`): warm off-white or near-black backgrounds, one purple accent, the blue, orange and aqua chart colors. Light and dark versions.
- Motion graphics with real charts. Plain sans-serif type. Captions on every video, because many people watch with the sound off.
- No stacks of cash, sports cars, rockets, money rain, trading screens with flashing tickers or "lifestyle" shots.

## How we talk about AI-made videos

People are right to be skeptical of AI content about money. Our answer is to be completely open about how the videos are made, and to be clear about what the review does and doesn't mean.

### What's true, so it's what we say

1. **AI drafts the script** from the curriculum's objectives and key points.
2. **Facts come only from the verified facts registry** (`invest/data/facts.json`): official sources, with dates.
3. **AI makes the video:** narrated motion graphics with real charts. Some videos use an AI voice; some are captions only. No fake human presenters, ever.
4. **An automatic check** (`invest/claims.py`) blocks hype, predictions and advice-style language.
5. **A person reviews and approves every video** before members see it. Unapproved videos show a "Draft" badge in the app.
6. **Every video carries the label "Made with AI, reviewed by a person."**

### The standard lines

- **Label (on screen and in captions):** "Made with AI, reviewed by a person."
- **One-sentence explanation:** "Our lesson videos are made with AI, and a person checks every one before it's published."
- **Why we use AI:** "It lets us keep the price low and update lessons quickly when rules change, like yearly contribution limits." Don't claim AI makes the lessons smarter, personalized or more accurate.
- **What "reviewed" means:** a person checks accuracy, sources and tone. It is not personal advice, and we don't describe the reviewer as a licensed professional. *(Founder: if anyone on the team holds a relevant license, decide with an attorney whether to mention it.)*

### Never

- Fake human avatars or presenters, or an AI voice that imitates a real person.
- AI-written testimonials, reviews, comments or "member stories".
- Calling the videos "expert-made", "certified" or "human-made".
- An AI chat that answers "what should I buy?" for a person. The AI stays behind the scenes, writing reviewed, impersonal lessons.
- Hiding the AI label when a clip is reposted, cut down or shared by a creator.

### Platform labels

On every clip, turn on the platform's own AI label in addition to our on-screen label and caption text.

- **TikTok** requires a label on AI-generated content that shows realistic images, audio or video, and encourages labeling anything completely generated or significantly edited by AI. Source: [TikTok Help Center, "How is AI-generated content labeled on TikTok?"](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content), checked 2026-11-15.
- **YouTube** requires creators to disclose realistic content made or meaningfully altered with AI. It shows a more prominent label for sensitive topics, and finance is one of them. Sources: [YouTube Help, "Disclosing use of GenAI content"](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491) and [YouTube blog, Mar 18, 2024](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content/), both checked 2026-11-15.
- **Instagram and Facebook:** use the AI label Meta offers in the posting flow. *(Not checked yet: confirm Meta's current rule before the first Reels post.)*

Our videos are animated rather than realistic, so some platforms may not strictly require a label. We label every clip anyway: it's the honest choice, and it removes any question about it.
