# Target segments: who to pitch the employer pilot to first

*Owner: marketing. Last updated 2026-11-14. This is guidance for finding the right **companies**. It is not a contact list, and nobody's personal contact data belongs in this repo.*

## Ideal customer profile (first 5 pilots)

| Trait | What we want | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Industry** | Restaurant groups, hotels with big food-and-beverage or resort teams, and retailers with hourly floor staff | Many young hourly workers and frequent quits |
| **Company size** | 5 to 50 locations and about 250 to 2,000 hourly staff in total (sweet spot 400 to 1,200) | Big enough to have an HR or payroll system and a comparison group. Small enough that one ops or HR leader can say yes without a long procurement process |
| **Pilot size** | At least 4 locations and 250+ eligible hourly staff; the program starts at about half the locations first | Our margin and the employer's payback both fall off below about 150 enrolled members, and a fair comparison needs locations that start later (see `docs/pilot/decisions.md`) |
| **Locations** | One state for the first pilots. Metros with colleges and tight hiring markets | One set of state wage and payroll rules to check. Lots of early-career workers |
| **Staff aged 18-25** | Employer estimates 30% or more | That's who the lessons are written for (see benchmarks below) |
| **Turnover pain** | They can name a number, e.g. "we lose half our new hires before 90 days" | A clear baseline makes the day-90 report useful |
| **Systems** | Payroll can add a one-time bonus line, and HR can export a weekly roster | The pilot needs both |

### How young are these workforces? (BLS benchmarks)

Our math from [BLS CPS Table 18b, 2025 annual averages](https://www.bls.gov/cps/cpsaat18b.htm), checked 2026-11-14. BLS groups ages as 16-19 and 20-24, so there's no exact 18-25 figure, and the 16-19 group includes teens under 18, who are **not** eligible for the pilot.

| Industry | Aged 20-24 | Aged 16-24 | Median age |
|---|---|---|---|
| Food services and drinking places | 21.5% | 41.3% | 29.2 |
| Clothing and accessories retailers | 22.1% | 33.4% | 31.5 |
| Grocery retailers (supermarkets) | 16.3% | 28.2% | 36.8 |
| Warehouse clubs, supercenters, general merchandise | 17.5% | 24.6% | 35.7 |
| Retail trade, all | 14.4% | 21.6% | 38.5 |
| Accommodation (hotels) | 10.4% | 14.0% | 43.6 |
| All industries | 8.8% | 12.2% | 42.1 |

**What this means:** restaurants and apparel retail are the best fit. Hotel workforces are older on average, so pitch hotels with large F&B, banquet or resort teams, and ask about the age mix before you invest time.

### Signs of turnover pain (all public, all at the company level)

- The same roles stay posted on the careers page or job boards for months, across several locations.
- Job ads mention sign-on bonuses, "immediate start" or "now hiring all shifts".
- "Now hiring" signs at several locations.
- New locations opening soon (local news, permits, the company's own posts).
- Leaders talking about staffing or retention in trade press or on LinkedIn.
- A big seasonal hiring wave (holiday retail, summer resorts, stadium seasons).
- They already pay referral or retention bonuses. They know the problem and will pay to fix it.

For context: accommodation and food services had 719,000 quits in September 2026, a 5.0% monthly rate, and retail trade had 475,000 (3.1%) ([BLS JOLTS, released Nov 3, 2026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/jolts_11032026.htm)). Leisure and hospitality has the lowest median tenure of any industry, 2.4 years ([BLS Employee Tenure, January 2026 data](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/tenure.nr0.htm)).

## Where to find them (public sources)

**Rule: research companies, not people.** Record the company name, category, public signals and how you reached them (LinkedIn, warm intro, event). Keep that tracker outside this repo. Don't build lists of individuals' names, emails or phone numbers, don't scrape or buy contact data, and honor every opt-out.

**Industry associations**
- **State restaurant associations.** The National Restaurant Association lists its state partner associations at [restaurant.org](https://restaurant.org/About/NRA-Partners/State-Restaurant-Associations) (found via search 2026-11-14; it describes 52 state associations, including DC and Puerto Rico). Good for member events, newsletters, sponsored talks and allied-member programs.
- **State hotel and lodging associations.** The American Hotel & Lodging Association lists its state partner associations on its [association members page](https://ahla.com/node/2782) (found via search 2026-11-14; not opened).
- **State retail associations.** Search "[state] retailers association" or "[state] retail federation". Many run HR and workforce committees.
- **Franchisee associations** for individual restaurant brands. Multi-unit franchisees are often the fastest decision makers.
- **Local chambers of commerce and business journals.** "Largest employers" and "fastest growing" lists name companies, not workers.

**Industry events** (dates need checking before you plan travel)
- National Restaurant Association Show, Chicago, May 22-25, 2027 (show website, via search 2026-11-14).
- NRF 2027: Retail's Big Show, New York, January 10-12, 2027 (secondary sources; **unverified**).
- State restaurant and hospitality shows, state lodging conferences, and local chapter meetings of HR professional groups. These are often cheaper and full of regional operators who fit our size.

**Picking markets**
- [BLS QCEW](https://www.bls.gov/cew/) and [Census County Business Patterns](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp.html) give county-level counts of restaurants, hotels and stores. Use them to pick one or two metros with lots of multi-unit operators.

**LinkedIn job titles to search** (search by title and company, then reach out through LinkedIn messages or a warm intro; don't export profiles)

| Restaurants | Hotels | Retail |
|---|---|---|
| Director or VP of Operations | General Manager | District Manager |
| Area or Regional Manager | Director of Human Resources | Regional Director of Stores |
| Director of People / HR | Talent and Culture Director | Store Operations Director |
| Franchise Owner / Operator | Director of Food and Beverage | HR Business Partner, Stores |
| Director of Training | Regional HR Director (management companies) | Director of Talent Acquisition |

| 11 | Waterparks, amusement parks and seasonal attractions | Very young, very seasonal | Many staff are under 18 and can't join |
| 12 | Family entertainment centers (bowling, trampoline parks, arcades) | Young hourly staff, multi-site owners | Small HR teams; keep setup light |
| 13 | Regional movie theater chains | Young part-time staff | Low weekly hours |
| 14 | Regional grocery chains | 28.2% of grocery workers are 16-24 (BLS 2025) | Union contracts may require bargaining over new pay programs |

## Pilot qualification checklist

Use this on the first call. **Must-haves** all need a yes.

**Must-haves**
- [ ] At least 250 eligible hourly staff aged 18 or older across at least 4 locations, willing to start at about half of them first.
- [ ] Payroll can add a one-time bonus line, and HR can send a weekly roster (employee ID, hire date, location, status). No Social Security numbers or pay rates needed.
- [ ] A named pilot owner in ops or HR with about 1 hour a week.
- [ ] Budget for the platform fee and a funded reward pool (DRAFT pricing: $4 per enrolled employee per month plus the pool).
- [ ] They accept that the day-90 report compares program locations with waiting locations, and that one pilot can't prove a small effect.
- [ ] Their counsel or HR adviser will review the DRAFT pilot terms, including tax and overtime treatment of rewards.
- [ ] No age cap on eligibility beyond 18+ (see the age-discrimination note in the one-pager FAQ).
- [ ] A decision maker who can sign within 30 days.

**Nice-to-haves**
- [ ] They can share last year's 90-day retention for the same locations and season.
- [ ] They have several similar locations, so we can stagger start dates for a fairer comparison.
- [ ] Their staff are 30% or more aged 18-25 (their estimate).
- [ ] Managers hold pre-shift huddles where the program can be mentioned.
- [ ] A hiring wave is coming, so new hires start the pilot near day 0.

**Disqualifiers (politely pass)**
- They want us to recommend investments, accounts or apps to staff.
- They want rewards tied to trading, number of trades or account balances.
- They want individual employees' personal data back in reports.
- They see the pilot as a replacement for fixing pay or schedules.
- Most staff are under 18.
- A union contract covers the staff and the union hasn't been consulted (ask counsel).
