# Employer outreach sequences

*Owner: marketing. Last updated 2026-11-14. Pricing in these messages is a DRAFT assumption ($4 per enrolled employee per month plus an employer-funded reward pool). Don't send until the founder approves the pilot terms.*

Three buyers, five touches each: a 3-email cold sequence, a LinkedIn connection note and follow-up, and a warm-introduction request. Every email body is under 120 words (counts exclude the subject line and the standard footer).

## Rules for every message

- **One real, public detail per message.** For example: they opened a new location, their careers page lists the same role at several sites, or they posted about staffing. Never fake familiarity.
- **No hype or pressure.** No urgency tricks, no fake deadlines, no promises that turnover drops. Say plainly that the day-90 report isn't a controlled experiment.
- **Contact data.** Use only a business email the person has published or given you, LinkedIn messaging, or a warm intro. Don't buy lists, scrape or guess personal emails, and don't store personal contact details in this repo.
- **CAN-SPAM applies to business email too.** The FTC says the law "makes no exception for business-to-business email." Every email needs a valid postal address and an easy way to opt out, and opt-outs must be honored within 10 business days ([FTC compliance guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business), checked 2026-11-14). Keep a do-not-contact list.
- **LinkedIn notes** are kept under 200 characters. The limit depends on account type (unverified; check before sending).
- **Spacing.** Email 2 about 4 business days after email 1, email 3 about 7 business days after email 2. Stop at the first reply of any kind.
- **Sources.** Every statistic in these emails comes from BLS: [JOLTS, Sept 2026 data, released Nov 3, 2026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/jolts_11032026.htm); [Employee Tenure, January 2026 data, released Sept 24, 2026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/tenure.nr0.htm); [CPS Table 18b, 2025 annual averages](https://www.bls.gov/cps/cpsaat18b.htm). Update the numbers when BLS publishes new data.

**Standard footer for every email:**

> [Your name] · Patient Money · pilot@example.com · [Postal address]
> Not interested? Reply "no" and I won't email again.

Attach the one-pager (`docs/marketing/employer-one-pager.md`, exported to PDF) only where an email says so.

## Buyer 1: Restaurant group operations or HR director

*Angle: crew who leave in the first 30 to 90 days. They care about labor cost, manager time and anything that slows a shift.*

#### Email 1 (day 1)

**Subject:** Crew who make it to day 90

Hi [First name],

Accommodation and food services had 719,000 quits in September, a 5.0% monthly rate (BLS, Nov 3). My guess is the first 90 days is where it hurts most at [Company].

We're running a 90-day pilot at one to three locations. Crew earn points for reaching 30, 90 and 180 days and for finishing two-minute money lessons. Points become a payroll bonus or gift card that you fund. At day 90 you get a report comparing enrolled and non-enrolled crew. It isn't a controlled study, and the report says so.

Draft pricing is $4 per enrolled employee per month plus your reward pool.

Worth a 20-minute call?

#### Email 2 (about day 5)

**Subject:** What your managers would actually do

Hi [First name],

A quick follow-up on what the pilot asks of [Company]'s managers, since that's usually the real cost:

- Hang one QR-code poster per location.
- Read a 60-second huddle script once.
- Send a weekly roster file: employee ID, hire date, location, status. No Social Security numbers or pay rates.

Here's the demo dashboard: [link to pilot/]. We never reward trading or account balances, and we don't give your crew investment advice.

Would [location or market] be a fair place to test it?

#### Email 3 (about day 12)

**Subject:** Should I close the loop?

Hi [First name],

I don't want to crowd your inbox, so this is my last note.

If early turnover isn't a top problem at [Company] right now, that's useful to know. If it is, the pilot is small: one location, 90 days, and a plain report at the end that says what we can and can't conclude.

The one-pager is attached. Reply "later" and I'll check back next quarter, or "no" and I'll stop here.

#### LinkedIn connection note

> Hi [First name], I work on keeping restaurant crew past their first 90 days with a small rewards-and-money-lessons pilot. I'd value connecting with someone who runs ops at [Company]'s scale.

#### LinkedIn follow-up (after they accept)

> Thanks for connecting, [First name]. No pitch deck. One question: at [Company], is the 30-to-90-day window where most crew turnover happens, or is it later? I'm running a 90-day pilot where crew earn points for tenure milestones and short money lessons, paid as a payroll bonus you fund. If it's relevant, I can send a one-page summary. If not, no reply needed.

#### Warm-introduction request (to a mutual contact)

> Hi [Mutual contact], would you be comfortable introducing me to [First name] at [Company]? I'm running a small 90-day pilot that rewards restaurant crew for tenure milestones and short money lessons, with an honest retention report at the end. I think [Company]'s [specific reason, e.g. new locations] makes it relevant. Here's a blurb you can forward, and no pressure either way:
>
> *"[Your name] runs a 90-day retention pilot for restaurant crew. Staff earn points for reaching 30, 90 and 180 days and for two-minute money lessons, paid as a payroll bonus or gift card the employer funds. It gives no investment advice and moves no money into investments. Contact: pilot@example.com."*

## Buyer 2: Hotel general manager or HR director

*Angle: housekeeping, food and beverage, front desk. They plan around a busy season. Note: hotels themselves employ fewer 20-24 year olds than restaurants (10.4% of the accommodation workforce vs. 21.5% in food services, BLS CPS 2025), so lead with properties that have large F&B, banquet or resort teams.*

#### Email 1 (day 1)

**Subject:** Housekeeping and F&B turnover at [Hotel]

Hi [First name],

Leisure and hospitality has the lowest median job tenure of any industry, 2.4 years (BLS, January 2026 data). For workers aged 20 to 24 in any job, it's 1.5 years.

We're piloting a small retention program for hotels. Hourly staff earn points at 30, 90 and 180 days and for two-minute money lessons, redeemed as a payroll bonus or gift card you fund. You get a dashboard and a day-90 report comparing enrolled and non-enrolled staff. It's an honest comparison, not a controlled study.

Draft pricing: $4 per enrolled employee per month plus your reward pool. Open to a 20-minute call?

**Subject:** Timing it around your season

One thought on timing. If [Hotel] staffs up before [peak season], a pilot that starts with that hiring wave gives the clearest read on whether new hires stay through the peak.

Setup is about two hours: a roster file (ID, hire date, department, status), a QR-code poster back of house, and a short huddle script. No Social Security numbers or bank details. Rewards go through your payroll as taxable wages.

Here's what staff would see: [link to pilot/member.html].

Would a pilot in [department] be worth discussing?

#### Email 3 (about day 12)

The one-pager is attached. Reply "later" and I'll check back after [peak season], or "no" and I'll stop.

## Answers to common replies

- **"Will this cut our turnover?"** "We don't know yet, and we won't claim it. The pilot is how we find out, and the report will say how sure we can be."
- **"We already pay retention bonuses."** "Then you already know the payroll side. What we add is the lessons, the tracking, and a report that compares joiners with non-joiners."
- **"Is this a 401(k) or investing product?"** "No. There are no accounts, no securities and no investment advice. It's points, lessons and a payroll bonus or gift card."
- **"Send me something."** Send the one-pager and the link to `employers.html`. Follow up once, a week later.
