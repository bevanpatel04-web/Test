# Consumer launch kit: the free calculator site

*Owner: marketing. Last updated 2026-11-14. For the founder to film and post. Audience: adults 18-25 only. Never target or address anyone under 18.*

## Rules for everything in this kit

- **Education, not advice.** No security, fund, coin, broker or app is named or recommended. Broad categories (index fund, 401(k), savings account) are fine.
- **No promised or predicted returns.** Every growth number is a labeled hypothetical with its assumptions, uses "could", and sits next to a risk line.
- **Sources on screen.** Every statistic shows its source and date on screen and in the caption.
- **On-screen label for every video:** "Education, not advice. Hypothetical, not a prediction." Keep it visible for at least 3 seconds.
- **Paid content:** if we're ever paid to mention anything, the post says "#ad" or "Paid partnership" right next to the mention. Nothing in this kit is paid.
- **Faceless option:** if the founder doesn't film to camera, each script works as a voice-over on top of screen recordings of the calculator.
- **Run every caption and script through the claims filter** before posting.

### Hypothetical numbers used below

All numbers come from the site's own calculator math (`invest/site/assets/calc.js`): the same amount added at the start of each month, a steady assumed yearly rate compounded monthly, no fees, taxes or inflation. Real investments don't grow at a steady rate and can lose money, including money you put in.

| Scenario (hypothetical) | Result at 65 | Total put in |
|---|---|---|
| $100/month from 21, assumed 6% a year | about $247,400 | $52,800 |
| $100/month from 26, assumed 6% a year | about $179,700 | $46,800 |
| From 21, assumed 4% a year | about $141,500 | $52,800 |
| From 21, assumed 8% a year | about $446,700 | $52,800 |
| From 21, assumed 6%, with a 35% drop at age 40 | about $184,600 (balance falls from about $41,800 to about $27,200 at 40) | $52,800 |
| From 21, assumed 5% (6% minus a 1% yearly fee) | about $186,200 | $52,800 |

## 10 short-video scripts (TikTok, Reels, Shorts)

Each script is under 100 spoken words, about 40 seconds at a normal pace. The hook goes on screen in the first 2 seconds, before the first spoken word ends.

### 1. Start at 21 or 26?

- **On-screen hook (0-2 s):** "Start investing at 21 or 26? I ran the math."
- **Spoken:** "I put two people into our free calculator. Both invest $100 a month until 65. One starts at 21, one at 26. I assumed 6% a year. That's an assumption, not a prediction. Starting at 21, it could reach about $247,000. Starting at 26, about $180,000. The early starter put in only $6,000 more. That's what time can do. But markets don't move in a straight line, and you can lose money you put in. Next video, I add a crash."
- **On screen:** calculator screen recording. Text: "Hypothetical: $100/month to age 65, assumed 6%/yr, no fees, taxes or inflation. Investments can lose value."
- **Caption:** "Same $100 a month, five years apart. Hypothetical math, not a prediction: investments can lose money. Try your own numbers, link in bio. Education, not advice."

### 2. Now add a crash

- **On-screen hook:** "Now let's crash it 35%."
- **Spoken:** "Same example: $100 a month from 21, an assumed 6% a year. Now I add a 35% drop at age 40. The balance falls from about $41,800 to about $27,200. That's about $14,600 gone on paper, in one hit. If you kept adding $100 a month after the drop, at the same assumed rate, you could end around $185,000 instead of $247,000. Real crashes can be deeper or last longer. A drop right before you need the money hurts most. That's why your timeline matters."
- **On screen:** the "Now add risk" toggle on the calculator. Text: "Hypothetical drop. Real crashes can be worse. Not a forecast."
- **Caption:** "What a 35% drop does to the 'start early' line. Hypothetical, and real crashes can be deeper. Education, not advice."

### 3. Why I'll never tell you what to buy

- **On-screen hook:** "I'll never tell you what to buy. Here's why."
- **Spoken:** "People ask me what stock to buy. I don't answer, on purpose. First, I don't know your debts, your timeline, or how you'd feel if it dropped 35%. Second, anyone who gets paid when you buy something should say so, and a lot of people online don't. So here's what I do instead: explain how investing works, show the math with numbers you pick, and put the risk right next to it. Your money, your risk, your call. For advice about your own situation, talk to a licensed professional."
- **On screen:** text cards for "1. I don't know your situation" and "2. Paid? Say so."
- **Caption:** "No stock tips here, ever. Here's what we do instead. Education, not advice."

### 4. Three things many people check first

- **On-screen hook:** "3 things many people check before investing."
- **Spoken:** "Three things many people check before they invest a dollar. One: high-interest debt. Credit card accounts that were charged interest averaged 22.36% APR last quarter, per the Federal Reserve. Paying that down saves interest for certain. Investment returns are never certain. Two: a cash cushion. Money you might need soon has no time to recover from a drop. Three: an employer match. If your job matches 401(k) contributions, your plan documents explain how. The full checklist is on our site."
- **On screen:** "22.36% avg APR, accounts charged interest, Q3 2026. Source: Federal Reserve G.19, Nov 6, 2026."
- **Caption:** "The 'before you invest' checklist, in 40 seconds. Full list at the link in bio. Education, not advice."

### 5. A 22% card vs. investing

- **On-screen hook:** "22% credit card vs. investing: the math."
- **Spoken:** "Say you have $1,000 on a credit card at 22.36%, the average APR on accounts charged interest, per the Federal Reserve. Left alone for a year, that balance costs you more than $200 in interest. Pay it off, and that cost is gone. That's a known saving. Put the same $1,000 into investments instead, and it might grow, or you might lose some of it. Nobody knows in advance. That's why a lot of people deal with expensive debt first. Your situation is yours. Just do the math before you pick."
- **On screen:** "22.36% avg APR, Q3 2026, Federal Reserve G.19. A $1,000 balance at 22.36% costs over $200 a year in interest."
- **Caption:** "Paying down a card is a known saving. Investing is not. Education, not advice."

### 6. Could you cover a surprise $400?

- **On-screen hook:** "Could you pay a surprise $400 bill with cash?"
- **Spoken:** "If a $400 bill showed up tomorrow, could you pay it with cash? In the Federal Reserve's latest survey, 63% of adults said they'd cover it with cash or its equivalent. So a lot of people would have to borrow, sell something, or couldn't pay. Here's why that matters for investing. If you invest money you might need next month and the market drops, you could be stuck selling at a loss. A cash cushion gives your investments time. How big? That depends on your life, not on a rule from a video."
- **On screen:** "63% would cover $400 with cash or equivalent. Federal Reserve SHED, fielded Oct 2025, released May 2026."
- **Caption:** "Why a lot of people build a cash cushion before investing. Education, not advice."

### 7. What a 1% fee adds up to

- **On-screen hook:** "What a 1% fee costs over 44 years."
- **Spoken:** "Fees come out every year, whatever the market does. Here's a hypothetical: $100 a month from 21 to 65. At an assumed 6% a year, you could end with about $247,000. Take 1% a year off for fees, so 5%, and it's about $186,000. That's a gap of about $61,000 from one percentage point. These rates are assumptions, not predictions, and real returns can be negative. But fees are one of the few things you can see in advance. Look for the fee line before you choose anything."
- **On screen:** "Hypothetical: assumed 6% vs. 5% a year (fee modeled as 1 point lower growth), $100/month, 21 to 65. Not a prediction."
- **Caption:** "One percentage point, 44 years. Hypothetical math; real returns can be negative. Education, not advice."

### 8. What a 401(k) match actually is

- **On-screen hook:** "What a 401(k) match actually is."
- **Spoken:** "If your job offers a 401(k) match, here's what it means. When you put part of your paycheck into the plan, your employer adds money too, up to a limit set by the plan. Every plan is different, so read your plan documents or ask HR. For 2027, the IRS says you can contribute up to $25,500 to a 401(k). The money in the plan is invested, so it can go down as well as up. And some matches vest over time, which means you may need to stay a while to keep them."
- **On screen:** "2027 401(k) limit: $25,500. Source: IRS, Nov 12, 2026."
- **Caption:** "401(k) match in plain English. Check your own plan's rules. Education, not advice."

### 9. One company vs. hundreds

- **On-screen hook:** "One company vs. hundreds: what's the difference?"
- **Spoken:** "What's the difference between a single stock and an index fund? A single stock is a piece of one company. If that company has a bad year, so does your money. A broad index fund holds lots of companies, sometimes hundreds or thousands, so one company's bad news matters less. That's diversification. It lowers one kind of risk, not all of it. When the whole market falls, a broad fund falls too. And funds charge fees every year. I'm not telling you which to pick. I'm telling you what you'd own."
- **On screen:** simple graphic, 1 box vs. a grid of 100 boxes. No logos or tickers.
- **Caption:** "Single stock vs. index fund, explained without picking either. Education, not advice."

### 10. Three red flags in money videos (mine included)

- **On-screen hook:** "3 red flags in money videos. Check mine too."
- **Spoken:** "Three red flags in money videos, and yes, check mine for them too. One: promised returns. Nobody knows what the market does next. Two: pressure to move fast. A real decision can wait a day. Three: getting paid without saying so. If someone earns money when you buy, they should say so clearly, right next to the pitch. Before you trust anyone selling investments, you can look them up for free at Investor.gov. And if one of my videos ever breaks these rules, call it out in the comments."
- **On screen:** three numbered text cards, then "Investor.gov (run by the SEC)".
- **Caption:** "Promised returns, pressure, hidden pay. Hold us to the same standard. Education, not advice."
