# Fundraising: when to raise, what proof to bring, and how Bilt did it

*Finance and ops, 2026-11-14. Round size and use of funds are **assumptions** to plan with, not advice on what to raise.*

## The short version

- **Don't raise yet.** Run 3 pilots first. They cost little: the employer funds rewards, and pilot fees cover our direct costs.
- **Raise when you can show:**
  - at least 2 employers paying after their pilot;
  - 3 to 5 signed letters of intent (LOIs) from other employers;
  - early retention data that points the right way.

  On the base case timeline that is about **months 6 to 9** after you start selling.
- **Assumed round:** $750K pre-seed on a SAFE. Carta's 2025 data shows a median valuation cap of about $10M for rounds of $250K to $1M. On that basis $750K would cost roughly 7% to 9% of the company. That is an assumption, not a quote.
- **What $750K buys:** about 18 months. That covers getting to roughly **$10K to $20K of monthly revenue (MRR)** and 6 to 12 months of retention data. In the base case we pass $10K MRR around month 19. Reaching $100K MRR takes about $1.25M of sales and marketing alone (model section 3), so plan on a seed round after this one.

## What proof investors will want, in order of strength

| Proof | Why it matters | Target before the pre-seed |
|---|---|---|
| **Paying customers** | Shows employers will pay, not just try | 2+ employers paying $4 per member per month after their pilot |
| **Retention data** | The whole value story. Bumped shut down in 2022 when its payers stopped seeing value | Members' 90-day new-hire retention vs a fair comparison group (staggered start or last year's numbers). Directional is fine; say so honestly |
| **LOIs or signed contracts** | Shows demand beyond friends | 3 to 5 LOIs naming price and size |
| **Enrollment and engagement** | Shows workers want it | 50%+ of eligible staff enrolled; 50%+ active in a month |
| **Unit economics** | Shows it can be a business | Our measured cost per member, gross margin (model: 62%), sales cycle and CAC |
| **Compliance plan** | Rewards, wages and later investing are regulated | Attorney-reviewed pilot agreement, member terms and privacy notice; no rewards for trading |
| **Pipeline** | Shows it can grow | 20+ qualified employers in conversation, with headcounts |

## How Bilt did it (verified 2026-11-14)

**Bilt signed the payers before the users.** It built an alliance of large landlords first. Then it offered renters rewards on rent.

- **Launch, June 22, 2021.** Bilt Rewards launched from the Kairos HQ startup studio. TechCrunch reported 15 of the top 20 owners involved and a rollout across "over 2 million rental units." Partners named in the press release include Blackstone, Equity Residential, AvalonBay, Related and Starwood Capital. ([TechCrunch, Sept 21, 2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/09/21/bilt-rewards-banks-60m-growth-on-a-350m-valuation-to-advance-credit-card-benefits-for-renters/); [PR Newswire, Sept 21, 2021](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/bilt-rewards-raises-60-million-from-wells-fargo-mastercard-and-the-nations-largest-real-estate-owners-to-scale-its-loyalty-program-and-credit-card-for-renters-301381447.html))
- **Sept 21, 2021: $60M at a $350M valuation**, which TechCrunch describes as "just 90 days from Bilt's launch."
  - **Correction to our brief:** June 22 to Sept 21 is 91 days. Say "about 90 days," not "less than 90 days."
  - The investors included Wells Fargo, Mastercard and the landlords themselves. **The payers became investors.**
- **July 10, 2025: $250M at a $10.75B valuation**, led by General Catalyst and GID, with United Wholesale Mortgage also investing. At that point Bilt said:
  - its network had signed 1 in 4 US apartment buildings;
  - the Bilt Alliance covered more than 4.5 million homes;
  - it expected to cross $1B in revenue by Q1 2026.

  ([Business Wire, July 10, 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250710461384/en/Bilt-Raises-%24250-Million-at-%2410.75-Billion-Valuation-to-Accelerate-Housing-and-Neighborhood-Commerce-Platform))

**What to copy:**
1. Sign a few large payers first: multi-unit restaurant and hotel operators.
2. Make the payer's ROI the headline, not the consumer's points.
3. Invite operators to invest as angels. An operator who invests also refers peers.

**What not to copy:**
- Bilt came out of a startup studio with deep investor networks. Its 2021 round was a growth round, not a typical pre-seed.
- Its revenue also depends on a credit card program. The team brief reports, **unverified here**, that its card bank lost up to $10M a month. Our model has no card and no bank partner in the pilot.

## Likely investor types

| Type | Why they fit | What they'll ask |
|---|---|---|
| **Fintech pre-seed funds** | Rewards that later become investing deposits through a licensed partner | Compliance, the path to the investing product, regulatory risk |
| **HR-tech and future-of-work pre-seed funds** | Retention for frontline workers is a known, painful problem | Proof of retention lift; payroll integrations; sales cycle |
| **Angels with restaurant or hospitality operating experience** (multi-unit franchisees, former COOs and HR leads of chains) | They know turnover costs first-hand and can open doors to other operators | "Would I buy this?" Manager time, how it fits payroll |
| **Strategic angels from payroll, scheduling or HR software** | Possible distribution partners later | Integration and channel ideas |
| **Later: credit-union-related investors** | Credit unions want young members (our second payer) | Member acquisition cost compared with their current channels |

**How to build the list:** find investors who led pre-seed or seed rounds in frontline workforce, earned wage access, employee benefits or rewards startups in the last 2 years. Check that each one still writes pre-seed checks before reaching out. We have not verified any specific fund here, so none are named.

## 10-slide pitch outline

1. **Title.** Patient Money (working name): "Rewards that keep young hourly workers, paid for by the employers who keep them."
2. **The problem.** Young hourly workers quit fast and replacing them is expensive. Accommodation and food services saw 719,000 quits in Sept 2026, a 5.0% monthly quit rate (BLS JOLTS). Replacing one worker costs roughly $1,500 to $6,000; we plan with $3,500 (research team range; CAP puts it at 16% of pay for jobs under $30,000).
3. **Who pays and why.** Employers, because the math works: the program costs about $9,700 a year for 150 members, so it pays for itself if about 3 people a year stay who would have left (a 3.5% cut in quits).
4. **The product.** Points for staying (30, 90, 180 and 365 days), for money lessons, and for healthy habits. Rewards are paid as a payroll bonus or a gift card. **Never** for trading or account balances.
5. **Why now and why us.** Gen Z wants to build wealth but distrusts hype. Our education-first brand (see `docs/patient-money-plan.md`) is the trust layer employers can offer. The compliance guardrails are built in from day one.
6. **Traction.** Pilots, enrollment and activity rates, early retention comparison, paying customers, LOIs. *(Fill in after the pilots.)*
7. **Business model.** $4 per enrolled member per month, $500 minimum. Rewards at cost. Measured gross margin (model: 62%), CAC and payback (model: about $5,000 and 13 months).
8. **Go-to-market.**
   - The Bilt playbook: sign employers first.
   - Start with multi-unit franchisees and regional restaurant and hotel groups with 250+ staff aged 18-25.
   - Then channel partners in payroll and scheduling software.
   - Then credit unions as a second payer.
9. **Competition and positioning.** Use the research team's competitor map in `docs/research/`. Position on measured retention ROI plus honest money education.
10. **Team, the ask and milestones.** $750K pre-seed (assumed). What it buys (table below). Milestones for the seed round: about $20K MRR, 30+ paying employers, 12-month retention data.

**Appendix slides:** legal guardrails; how points become investing deposits later through a licensed partner (the member chooses; we never pick); model assumptions with ranges.

## Timeline (base case, month 1 = first month of selling)

| Months | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 to 3 | Sign Pilot 1 (design partner); line up Pilots 2 and 3; attorney reviews the drafts |
| 4 to 6 | Pilots run; collect 30-day and 90-day data |
| 6 to 9 | First conversions to paid; collect LOIs; **raise the pre-seed** |
| 9 to 18 | First salesperson; 10 to 20 paying employers; 12-month retention data from the first pilots |
| 18 to 24 | **Seed round** on retention proof and about $20K MRR (model base: $20K MRR around month 24) |
