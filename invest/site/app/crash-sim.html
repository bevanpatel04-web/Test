Assumptions: selling happens at the lowest point, cash earns nothing while you wait, and taxes and fees are left out. In real life, nobody knows where the bottom is while it's happening.

What this shows, and what it doesn't

  • It shows how the timing of selling and buying back changes the result in one example. It doesn't predict what any real market does next.
  • In these made-up scenarios the index gets back to where it started. Real recoveries can take much longer, and an investment that falls doesn't have to come back.
  • Money you might need soon has no time to wait out a drop, which is one reason people keep a cash cushion.

Education, not advice. The crash simulator uses a pretend portfolio and hypothetical numbers. It isn't a forecast, it doesn't use real money, and it doesn't recommend buying, selling or holding anything. Investing involves risk, including losing money you put in. Past performance doesn't guarantee future results.
