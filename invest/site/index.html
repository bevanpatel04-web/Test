Before you invest: what many people check first

  1. High-interest debt. Credit card accounts that were charged interest averaged APR (). Paying down a balance saves that interest for certain. Investment returns are never certain.
  2. A cash cushion. of adults said they'd cover a surprise $400 expense with cash or its equivalent (). Money you might need soon has no time to recover from a drop.
  3. An employer match. If your job offers a 401(k) match, it's extra pay for contributing. Your plan's documents say how it works. You can contribute up to to a 401(k) in 2027 ().
  4. The account type. A 401(k), a Roth or traditional IRA and a regular brokerage account have different tax rules. The 2027 IRA contribution limit is ().
  5. What you'd actually own. A single stock rides on one company. A broad index fund spreads your money across hundreds of them. Fees come out every year, whatever the market does.
  6. Red flags. Promised returns, pressure to act fast, and people who get paid when you buy but don't say so. You can look up anyone selling investments at Investor.gov.
