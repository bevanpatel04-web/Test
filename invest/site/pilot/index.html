What this comparison shows, and what it doesn't

  • It shows how many people in each group were still on staff each month. Both lines start at 100% when the pilot began.
  • It doesn't prove the rewards caused the gap. This isn't a controlled experiment. People who choose to join may be different from people who don't. They might already plan to stay longer, work more hours or feel more settled in the job.
  • Other things matter too, like location, role, schedule, pay and the time of year.
  • Small groups swing a lot. In a group of 100 people, one person leaving moves the line by a full point.
  • A fairer test: start the program at some locations first, picked at random, and compare them with the rest over the same months.
